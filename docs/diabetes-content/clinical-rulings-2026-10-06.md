# Diabetes Care: clinical rulings, 2026-10-06

You asked for the clinical questions (C1–C45 in [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md)) to be answered from verified Canadian sources. This file records the answers, one ruling per question, for your sign-off as the nurse.

**How the rulings were made.** For each question, a researcher fetched the sources and quoted their exact words. An independent verifier then re-opened every source, checked each quote word for word, and either confirmed the ruling or corrected it. Where they differ, the verifier's version is the one recorded here. A source counts only if it was opened on 2026-10-06; search snippets and pages that would not open do not count, and are named where that matters. When Canadian sources disagree, both are shown side by side (see "Where Canadian sources disagree").

Nothing in the live site has been changed yet. Each ruling lists what would change. The exact English and French copy and the register entries are in the fold-out under each ruling.

## Summary

- **All 45 questions have a ruling.** The verifier confirmed 17 and corrected 28. Corrections were to wording, scope or source bookkeeping. None was refuted.
- **35 are clinical and 10 are editorial.** The editorial ones (C12, C22, C23, C25, C29, C30, C31, C33, C40, C44) are choices about presentation, labels, links and pinning. Where they touch a fact, that fact has a Canadian source.
- **32 of the 35 clinical rulings are settled from Canadian sources** fetched and quoted today. Most rest on Diabetes Canada (guidelines and patient pages), Health Canada or other Canadian national bodies. Two rest partly on product documents: C7 on Health Canada–authorized product monographs, which the makers write, and C42 on Tandem's Canadian user guide, an industry source (only makers state which devices work together).
- **Nothing is left unsettled, but 3 rulings have a source gap: C32, C34, C35.** These are sharps return outside the HPSA provinces, the name "type 3c", and Wolfram syndrome. Each keeps the protective default: an instruction that makes no claim, or the line stays held. There are two smaller gaps inside settled rulings. In C10, the "go to the emergency department" fallback for sudden eye changes is a protective default that the eye sources do not state. In C11, "today" is the protective reading of the guideline's "should not be delayed".
- **Some choices are still yours.** They are listed under "Choices left to you". Each one has a safe default already in the ruling.

### Lows and ketones

#### C1. What number defines a low: below 3.9 or below 4.0 mmol/L?

**Ruling.** Keep the default: below 3.9 mmol/L defines a low everywhere (card 1, adult and child re-treat, NTJ ruler, time-in-range floor). Only the driving card stays at 4.0, with its visible note.

**Why.** Every current Canadian definition of a low uses 3.9 (CPG Ch14 2023, Ch41 2025, Ch9 2021, the DC Hypoglycemia page, Diabète Québec 2025). The only current Canadian use of 4.0 is the driving guidance (CPG Ch21), where it is the more protective number.

**Sources.**

- Diabetes Canada, Clinical Practice Guidelines, [Chapter 14: 2023 Update – Hypoglycemia in Adults](https://www.diabetes.ca/for-professionals/full-guidelines/chapter-14-2023-update): "retreated with 15 g of carbohydrate if BG remains &lt;3.9 mmol/L"
- Diabetes Canada, Clinical Practice Guidelines, [Glycemic Management Across the Lifespan for People With Type 1 Diabetes (Ch41, 2025)](https://www.diabetes.ca/for-professionals/full-guidelines/chapter-41): "retreated with 5–15 g of carbohydrate if able to swallow and blood glucose remains &lt;3.9 mmol/L"
- Diabetes Canada, [Hypoglycemia (Low blood sugar)](https://www.diabetes.ca/living-with-diabetes/blood-sugar-management/hypoglycemia): "(less than 3.9 mmol/L), it is called low blood sugar or hypoglycemia"
- Diabète Québec, [Low Blood Sugar - Symptoms and Actions to Take (leaflet, 2025)](https://www.diabete.qc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/112382-DQC25-Depliants6-Hypoglycemie-EN_11-2025_web.pdf): "Your blood sugar is less than 3.9 mmol/L, with or without symptoms"
- Diabetes Canada, Clinical Practice Guidelines, [Diabetes and Driving (Ch21)](https://www.diabetes.ca/for-professionals/full-guidelines/chapter-21): "Should not drive when their BG level is &lt;4.0 mmol/L" (supports the driving exception only)

**What changes on the site.** No wording change. The record (SS R1, NTJ R1, YT R19, EDL R1/D1) and the sources-review notes are closed. Optionally add the Diabète Québec leaflet to SS card 1 so French readers have a French source for 3.9.

**Flags.** Source gap: no · Editorial: no · Confidence: high · Verifier: confirmed

#### C4. Who is the ketone ladder for, and how strong is its top rung?

**Ruling.** Keep the ladder visible to everyone, labelled 'written for type 1'. Blood rung 4 (over 3.0) and urine 'large' say 'Medical emergency. Get emergency care now'. The note adds DC's line that high ketones need medical care right away with any type. Also give blood rung 3 and urine 'moderate' an emergency-department fallback for when the team can't be reached. This matches card 8 and the DC sick-day sheet.

**Why.** Breakthrough T1D calls the top rung a medical emergency, and Diabetes Canada says high ketones with any type need care right away. The verifier added an emergency-department fallback to the 1.5–3.0 / moderate rung, because Diabetes Canada's sick-day sheet sends moderate ketones to the team "and/or" the ED and a reader who cannot reach the team at night should not wait.

**Sources.**

- Breakthrough T1D Canada, [Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and ketones (modified 2026-08-31)](https://breakthrought1d.ca/daily-management/diabetic-ketoacidosis-dka-and-ketones/): "More than 3.0 mmol/L is considered a medical emergency and indicates that you should call your diabetes care team immediately and possibly go to the emergency room"
- Diabetes Canada, [Hyperglycemia](https://www.diabetes.ca/living-with-diabetes/blood-sugar-management/hyperglycemia): "If you have very high blood sugar or high levels of ketones, you should seek medical treatment right away."
- Diabetes Canada, [Stay Safe When You Have Diabetes and Are Sick or at Risk of Dehydration](https://www.diabetes.ca/getContentAsset/5bdc8b7c-4402-4d72-b86b-700f9dfd3b9d/0f6cf596-933c-4f74-b36a-77091c512445/stay-safe-when-you-have-diabetes-and-sick-or-at-risk-of-dehydration.pdf?language=en): "If you have been told to check your ketones and they are moderate to high"
- Diabetes Canada, [Stay Safe When You Have Diabetes and Are Sick or at Risk of Dehydration](https://www.diabetes.ca/getContentAsset/5bdc8b7c-4402-4d72-b86b-700f9dfd3b9d/0f6cf596-933c-4f74-b36a-77091c512445/stay-safe-when-you-have-diabetes-and-sick-or-at-risk-of-dehydration.pdf?language=en): "Call your health-care team … and/or go the Emergency Department"
- Diabetes Canada, Clinical Practice Guidelines, [Glycemic Management Across the Lifespan for People With Type 1 Diabetes (Ch41, 2025)](https://www.diabetes.ca/for-professionals/full-guidelines/chapter-41): "ketone testing supplies, and knowing when to seek medical help"

**What changes on the site.** Staying Safe card 7. Top blood rung (over 3.0) and urine "large" become "Medical emergency. Get emergency care now". Blood 1.5–3.0 and urine "moderate" become "…Call your team now. If you can’t reach them, go to the emergency department". The card note adds that, with any type, high ketones need medical care right away.

#### C45. KYT cards 6 and 8: the "not overweight" wording

**Ruling.** Card 6 clue takes the CDE wording, 'you have a lower body weight'. Card 8 (MODY) uses Diabetes Canada's own phrase, 'not having obesity'.

**Why.** For the card 6 type clue, CPG Ch3 describes type 1 as "usually thin", so "lower body weight" fits. For MODY, Ch3 says "not having obesity" and gives weight as similar to the general population, so "lower body weight" would overstate that clue. Ch3 also says age at diagnosis and time to insulin predict type 1 better than BMI, so weight stays a soft clue.

**Sources.**

- Diabetes Canada, Clinical Practice Guidelines, [Definition, Classification and Diagnosis of Diabetes, Prediabetes and Metabolic Syndrome (Ch3, 2018)](https://www.diabetes.ca/for-professionals/full-guidelines/chapter-3): "onset &lt;25 years, not having obesity"
- Diabetes Canada, Clinical Practice Guidelines, [Definition, Classification and Diagnosis of Diabetes, Prediabetes and Metabolic Syndrome (Ch3, 2018), Table 2](https://www.diabetes.ca/for-professionals/full-guidelines/chapter-3): "Usually thin, but, with obesity epidemic, can have overweight or obesity"

**What changes on the site.** KYT 6.s2.1: "You were diagnosed at a younger age, or you have a lower body weight". KYT 8.items.3: "Clues include being diagnosed young, not having obesity, having no type 1 antibodies, and a parent with diabetes, in two or more generations".
