## 2. Safety and scope problems

### Clinical safety (fix before sign-off)

**S1 (high): chest pain during exercise (3.items.6).**
- The copy, "Stop and speak to your doctor if you're short of breath or have chest pain", follows the source but can delay emergency care for a heart attack. People with diabetes often have atypical or silent ischemia.
- The chapter's `urgentExit` points to Staying Safe `#red-flags`. That block covers only severe lows and DKA (unconscious or seizure, vomiting, ketones, Kussmaul breathing). It has no chest pain, stroke or sudden vision loss. So the signpost "Signs that need emergency care are in Staying Safe" overpromises for this chapter.
- **Recommend:**
  - (a) Register Heart & Stroke Foundation "signs of heart attack / FAST" and add a 9-1-1 line to card 3 ("Stop right away. If chest pain, pressure or shortness of breath doesn't ease quickly with rest, call 9-1-1") and to card 11 (release held item E1).
  - (b) Until then, reword `urgentExit.lead` so it only claims what Staying Safe covers, e.g. "Emergencies with lows and highs are in Staying Safe, under…".
  - Nurse ruling D4 should go to "call 9-1-1", not "source wording".

**S2 (medium): severe low while driving (7.items.8).**
- The Ch21 rec says the driver "must refrain from driving immediately".
- **Add:** "Don't drive again until your health-care provider says you can." This matters more than the notification line.

**S3 (medium): alcohol (4.items.3).**
- The sheet's "call an ambulance if you pass out" is missing.
- **Add:** "…and to call 9-1-1 if you pass out". This matches Staying Safe red-flag sign 1.
- Keep out the sheet's "glucagon will not work while alcohol is in the body". It is outdated and conflicts with Staying Safe's glucagon advice.

**S4 (medium): feet (8.items.7).**
- The "right away" list has no open sore or ulcer, and no infection signs.
- Ch32 rec: "a foot ulcer or show signs of infection even in the absence of pain should be treated promptly".
- **Add:** "a sore, blister or cut that isn't healing, or signs of infection, even if it doesn't hurt."
- Nurse to consider: a red, hot, swollen foot with fever or feeling unwell needs same-day care.

**S5 (low): eyes (9.items.6).**
- "Right away" matches CNIB ("immediately").
- Nurse to consider: sudden loss of vision, a curtain or shadow, or a sudden shower of floaters needs same-day emergency eye care. This is not in the registered sources.

**S6 (low): mental health (card 5).**
- The crisis strip is correct: 9-8-8 by call or text, 24/7, and 9-1-1 if safety is at risk.
- The "rather than waiting it out" route is appropriate.
- No other issue.

### Scope
- No dosing, titration, insulin-to-carb maths, time-zone unit changes, in-use insulin days or alcohol limits appear in the copy. All are correctly left out.
- No drug class is named:
  - The heart "D" says only "Drugs to protect your heart"; the DC page's ACE inhibitor, ARB, statin, Aspirin and clopidogrel are left out.
  - Ch29's SGLT2i/kidney-medicine message is left out.
  - "insulin secretagogue" is rendered as "a diabetes pill that can cause lows". This is acceptable.
- No product or brand names. MedicAlert (DC Exercise, alcohol sheet), Omnipod 5 (CATSA) and Life Savers/Rocket (Drive Safe card) are all left out. The pharmacist panel promotes Liivv's own CDE service, not a product.
- 2.items.4 states carbohydrate ranges. They are sourced and framed as "for most people" with a dietitian line. This is acceptable, pending D6.

### Canadian accuracy and labelling
- Every cited source is Canadian. Nothing needs, or carries, an "International guidance" label, and none is mislabelled.
- The names are correct: 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline, Breakthrough T1D, CATSA, Disability Tax Credit, Canada's Guidance on Alcohol and Health, Canada's food guide, and licensing by province/territory. mmol/L is used throughout.
- **S10 (low):** "ABCDES" should be "ABCDEs" (DC patient page) or "ABCDESSS" (CPG). Applies to the title and 11.items.2.
- **S7:** alcohol framing (4.items.1 and 4.items.5). DC's current guideline (Ch18 2023) is stricter than the 2018 sheet and the 2019 article that 4.items.1 relies on. Present the 2023 position alongside them (see rows 4.1 and 4.5).
