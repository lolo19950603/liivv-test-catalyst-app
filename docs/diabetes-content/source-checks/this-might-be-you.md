## 2. Safety, scope and labelling

**Safety**
- **S1 (fix before publishing): card 3 has no severe-low emergency step.** Item 7 covers glucagon but leaves out what both cited school sources put first: call 911, and don't leave the student alone. Diabetes@School says "Have someone call 911… Stay with the student until ambulance arrives" and "911 should be called even before giving glucagon". CPS says "a student must not be left alone when hypoglycemia is suspected. In the event of severe hypoglycemia, school personnel should call 911." Add an item: "Never leave a student alone with a low. If a student can't swallow safely, is unresponsive or has a seizure, staff call 911 first, then give glucagon if the plan includes it." Make card 3 `urgentContent: true`, since school staff are a stated audience, and add "Who calls 911" to the care-plan figure.
- **S2: 1.s2.4** should read "normal or only a little high" (Ch 15). Otherwise a normal meter reading might wrongly reassure someone.
- **S3: 1.s3.1** should add the risk of lows after delivery ("check more often in the first days"). This is in the source and needs no dose.
- **S4: 4.items.3** reads as a fixed target for frail people. The source says "may be considered" and only for people on medicines that can cause lows. Add "Your team sets yours" so readers don't relax their own targets.
- **S5: 1.s1.4.** Consider adding "Don't stop any medicine on your own", alongside the medicine review.
- The chapter's urgent exit goes to `staying-safe#red-flags`. Nothing in the copy delays emergency care, apart from the gaps in S1 and S2. 9-8-8 is correct.

**Scope (no dosing or titration, no drug classes, no product recommendations)**
- No doses, titration, drug names or drug classes in the copy. Folic acid amount, pregnancy targets, the 50% insulin cut and the Ch 37 drug lines are all left out, as the M-rulings intend. The children's carbohydrate grams are only referenced (The Rule of 15).
- **C1:** 4.items.5 names a device format (prefilled pens) as the guideline's example, with no brand. This is acceptable, but it is close to a device recommendation, so it's for the nurse to rule on (M8).
- No brand names in the message copy. 6.items.2 uses generic terms: fix "flash glucose monitor" as noted. No product strips.

**Canadian accuracy**
- The NIHB eligibility exceptions (BC/FNHA, Nunavik, James Bay Cree, Nunatsiavut, Nisga'a and others) make 6.items.1 and 6.items.5 inaccurate for several regions. This widens blocker M11.
- MB isn't confirmed as having a separate paediatric pump program on the cited page.
- Quebec pump age rule: confirmed current.
- DTC wording "automatically": change to "meets… for 2021 and later tax years" and note that a practitioner certifies the form.
- Ontario $170: limited to people 65+, on daily insulin, living at home.
- Terminology: CGM rather than "flash"; Disability Tax Credit, RDSP, NIHB, ADP and 9-8-8 are all correctly named.

**"International guidance" labelling**
- No non-Canadian source is used, so no "International guidance" label is needed. FAIL sources (Hart 2016, ADA 2026) and FIT are not cited.

**Diabetes Express**
- Not mentioned or linked anywhere in the copy or the meta.
