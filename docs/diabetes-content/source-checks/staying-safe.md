## C) Safety and scope problems

**Safety**

1. **Glucagon card, order of actions (3.items.3, urgent.signs.1).** The copy says "after giving it… call 911". Diabetes@School says to have someone call 911 first, then give glucagon. Write it so 911 is never delayed: "Call 911 (or have someone call), give glucagon if you have it, turn them on their side, stay with them, and don't put food or drink in their mouth."
2. **20 g line (3.items.4)** is missing "with severe signs". As written it reads as a rival to the 15 g rule for any low.
3. **"Can't keep fluids down" is routed three different ways.** urgent.signs.2 says 911 or ED. Band card 3, 8.s4.1 and 11.items.3 say team **or** ED. A reader in a hurry may pick "call the team" and wait. Choose one: either keep it in the red flags and make the others say "go to the emergency department, or call your team right away if you can reach them now", or drop it from the red flags per the sheet (R7). My ruling is to keep it as a red flag and align the others. Vomiting with no fluids kept down on insulin is an ED situation.
4. **Missing red flag: DKA symptoms.** The red-flag block only covers ketone numbers. A person with no meter is not covered. Breakthrough lists DKA symptoms ("breathing more deeply than usual", "breath that smells fruity", "stomach pain, feeling sick or being sick", "feeling tired, sleepy or confused") and says DKA "requires immediate medical attention". The DC sick-day sheet lists "difficulty breathing". Suggest urgent.signs.4: "Has deep or hard breathing, fruity-smelling breath, or is very drowsy or confused with a high blood sugar". This also releases the E row on DKA symptoms.
5. **Children on automated insulin delivery (AID).** The 5/10/15 g table is for injections or a non-AID pump only. Ch41 gives 5/5/5–10 g for AID, and 5–10 g for all ages on AID. The copy says "injections or a pump", which includes AID pumps. This needs fixing before publishing.
6. **Honey with the child toggle.** The 15 g list includes honey and stays visible when "A child" is selected, including the under-5 row. Honey must not be given to babies under 12 months. Add "Not for babies under 1 year" to the honey option, or hide honey when the child toggle is on. This is general clinical knowledge, not from the cited sources, so the nurse needs to sign it off.
7. **Nasal glucagon "from age 4" (3.note).** It is accurate for the ≥4 band, but Ch41 allows nasal glucagon under 4 when injectable is unavailable, and says injectable "availability is limited in Canada". Don't imply a child under 4 can't get it. Send families of children under 4 to their team.
8. **Auto-injector (3.items.2).** I couldn't confirm it is available in Canada. Listing a form people can't get can lead to confusion in an emergency. Say "nasal spray or injection" and route the rest to the pharmacist.
9. **Driving 40 minutes (2.note).** "Up to 40 minutes" is weaker than CPG Ch21's "at least 40 minutes after… at least 5.0". Use the CPG wording.
10. **Sick-day fluids (8.s2.1).** The copy is missing "unless you have been told to limit fluids".
11. **Alcohol (card 4).** The DC Alcohol and diabetes PDF says "glucagon… will not work while alcohol is in the body… make sure that someone knows to call an ambulance if you pass out." Worth adding to card 4, or as a clause in card 3, because the glucagon card otherwise implies glucagon always works.
12. **911 and 988.** 911 is used correctly: for can't swallow, unconscious or seizure, and as the alternative to the ED for the other red flags. 988 does not appear, which is correct. It is the suicide crisis line and has no role here. No line delays emergency care except the inconsistency in #3.

**Scope**

1. **Drug-class list in card 8 (8.s3.1–2).** It names metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics and NSAIDs as medicines to stop. It is framed as a quote of the DC sheet, but on the page it still reads as drug-class stop advice, which this review's brief excludes. Stopping a diuretic or an ACE inhibitor is not safe for every reader. Recommend R4 option (a): drop the list, keep "the sheet lists medicines some people pause when they're sick; your pharmacist will tell you which of yours" and the blank plan line. That matches the sheet's own "Ask your pharmacist to tell you" box. If the owner keeps the list, add the sheet's note that combination pills are not listed.
2. **Card 9.items.1.** As worded, "giving insulin by pen or syringe" with no team qualifier in the first sentence is close to treatment instruction from a clinician-facing, manufacturer-run source. Reword as above so it rests on "your team's backup plan". On R9, the FIT site is © Embecta Corp. I would count it as industry-run. Hold 9.1 and 9.3 until a non-industry second source is found. DC Technology & Devices supports "backup… rapid-acting insulin pens or syringes… ketone testing strips". DC Emergency Situations supports "learn how to switch to injections". Those two can support a re-worded 9.1 without FIT.
3. **FIT disclosure (9.note).** A disclosure is present, but "published with support from embecta" understates the link. See the correction above.
4. **Breakthrough DKA page.** It now has a "with support from Abbott" story campaign at the foot. Abbott makes blood ketone meters and strips. The ketone ranges themselves cite DC CPG Ch15 and are editorial. No disclosure is needed in the copy, but record it in sources-review.
5. **Dosing and titration.** There are no insulin doses, glucagon doses or adjustments in the copy. That passes. If glucagon doses are ever added, use the sources exactly: nasal 3 mg; injectable 1 mg SC/IM for adults (CPG Ch14 and the DC 02/24 sheet), and 0.5 mg if <20 kg in children (Ch41). Never a single injectable dose for all ages.
6. **Drug classes elsewhere.** Card 6 says only "some diabetes medicines". That passes.
7. **Product placements and Diabetes Express.** None in the copy. Baqsimi, Dex4, MiniMed and Dexcom appear only in comments and in the E/held table. That passes. The Liivv CDE lane is a service, not a product.
8. **"Written for type 1" label.** It is present in the figure (`writtenFor`) and in the note. That passes. Breakthrough's page confirms its ranges are "for people with T1D".
9. **Wrong-source citations to fix in SOURCE_META.** 4.items.3, 4.items.4 and 5.items.3 should cite the DC Alcohol and diabetes PDF, not the 2019 article. 10.items.4 should cite dq-all-about-injections. 1.note should cite dc-drive-safe-card. 4.items.2 should be reworded to the page.
10. **Held items (section E) that can now be released with a source:** nothing by mouth (das-glucagon); DKA symptoms and "ketones build up when there isn't enough insulin" (bt1d-dka-and-ketones); fast sugar in the emergency kit (dc-managing-emergency-situations); medical ID in general (dc-exercise-and-activity + DC Alcohol PDF); glucagon expiry ("Routinely check your prescription's expiration date", bt1d-what-is-glucagon).
