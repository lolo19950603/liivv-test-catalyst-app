## B) Safety problems

1. **Card 1 crisis strip has no 9-1-1.** The strip carries only 9-8-8. The 9-8-8 page itself says "If your safety is at risk, call 9-1-1 right away." The strip already supports an emergency line (`{ tel: '911', kind: 'emergency' }` → "Emergency: call 911"). Add it so the strip never sends someone in immediate danger to a counselling line alone.
2. **Card 5 glucagon item has no emergency step.** 5.items.3 teaches support persons to give glucagon but stops there. The same Ch14 recommendation says caregivers "should call for emergency services". Suggested: "…should be taught how to give glucagon, and to call 9-1-1. What to do in an emergency is in Staying Safe." Link the second sentence to `staying-safe#red-flags`.
3. **The low-band link label promises the wrong card.** `figure.ruler.lowLink` reads "What to do about a low: Know your low" and points to Staying Safe card 1. Card 1 defines a low; the steps are on card 2 (The Rule of 15). Fix one or the other: label "What counts as low: Know your low" (card 1), or point the link at card 2 and label it "What to do about a low: The Rule of 15".
4. **9.items.4 "In those provinces…"** implies sharps can go in the garbage elsewhere. HPSA's statement is unqualified. Drop "In those provinces".
5. **Children.** Card 3 presents adult targets as "Diabetes Canada's usual targets". Ch9's sensor targets explicitly exclude children and adolescents, and DC's patient page says targets vary for children. Add "children" to 3.items.5. Band cards 3 and 4 need a children qualifier (as A above).
6. **TIR floor 3.9 vs 4.0.** DC's patient page uses 4.0–10.0. Fold this into the nurse's R1 ruling so the ruler, Staying Safe and this card agree.
7. **Urgent exit.** This is correct. `urgentExit: { chapter: 'staying-safe' }` resolves through `DIABETES_SITE.anchors.redFlags` to `staying-safe#red-flags`. The lead says it covers lows and highs. Nothing in the chapter delays emergency care. The 4.items.6 "talk to your doctor before…" concerns starting exercise, not symptoms.
8. **No time limit on the card 1 "if it isn't easing" route.** This is acceptable, because the strip (with 9-1-1 added) covers crisis.

## C) Scope, labelling and Canadian accuracy

1. **3.items.2 (5.0–8.0 after meals if A1C isn't at target)** is the nearest thing to titration in the chapter. Stated bare, it invites a reader to tighten their own targets. Ch8 says it "may be considered, but must be balanced against the risk of hypoglycemia". Suggested: "If your A1C isn't at target, your team may set a lower after-meal target. Diabetes Canada gives 5.0 to 8.0." Alternatively, drop it from Ch01.
2. **No dosing, titration or drug-class advice.** Confirmed. The messages contain no doses, units, drug names or classes. Glucagon is named without a dose. "Your prescriber chooses your insulin/medicines" is present. 6.items.3 names a device type with "ask your team" (N6 is acceptable).
3. **No product or brand names.** Confirmed in the messages; MedicAlert® is correctly dropped. The Liivv pharmacist-CDE lane and panel are a service, not a product. Their hours and scope are owner claims; confirm them with the owner.
4. **Diabetes Express.** It is not mentioned or linked in the messages or meta. Section E of the draft holds an unconfirmed phone number "1-844-561-1254 for Markham" from a "2024 list". Before it is ever released, confirm it is a Liivv number and not a Diabetes Express one.
5. **"International guidance" label.**
   - The draft says no international source is cited. That is true of the SourceIds: all are Canadian (DC, Health Canada, 9-8-8, Ontario, HPSA, CDECB, Breakthrough T1D Canada).
   - However, 3.items.4 calls an International Consensus target "Canadian targets". Reword it as in A. No label is needed, because DC's guideline adopts the target, but the word "Canadian" must go.
   - Note: bt1d-time-in-range carries "made possible by support from the Time in Range Coalition. The diaTribe Foundation retains strict editorial independence". It is a US-origin, coalition-funded page. It should not be the sole source for anything; here it is a secondary source only.
   - TrialNet is an international research network offered in Canada via Breakthrough T1D Canada. Calling it "free research screening" is accurate.
6. **Canadian terms are correct.** These include mmol/L, A1C, 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline, Canada's food guide, Health Products Stewardship Association, CDE®, Ontario Drug Benefit, and Breakthrough T1D (formerly JDRF).
7. **Cross-chapter names.** The copy names Every Day Living, Your Tools, This Might Be You, Know Your Type and Funding & Coverage. Know Your Type has no route yet (N12). The current `chapters-data.ts` still numbers New to the Journey "03" and Every Day Living "02". Check that the urgentExit link text "Chapter 02 — Staying Safe" matches the shipped numbering.
